Ilya Boxers Institute is a Moscow-born research institute and apparel house devoted to the study and improvement of boxer shorts. The Institute maintains a public index of the garment, publishes field notes from its standing panel, and convenes fellows who take the unseen layer seriously. Founded in Moscow and worn elsewhere, it treats underwear as infrastructure: unglamorous, consequential, overlooked until it fails. Our method is simple: observe the wearer, not the mirror; record the day, not the pose; revise the finding when the evidence changes. The Collection applies that research with quiet restraint. Same initials. New subject. The standard remains.

## 5. The Collection — Product Concepts

### The Index

**Positioning:** The reference boxer short.

The garment against which the others are measured. A plain, mid-rise boxer short with a clean waistband and enough room for a long sitting. Cut to disappear under tailoring; finished to survive the second glance. Worn by the panel as a control, and by everyone else as a preference.

### The Quorum

**Positioning:** A lounge short for deliberations conducted horizontally.

A relaxed short for the hours when decisions are made lying down. Wider through the leg, softer at the waist, with a drawcord that asks rather than instructs. Suitable for reading, receiving bad news, and remaining seated through both. The Institute recommends it for any meeting that could have been a memo.

### The Field Standard

**Positioning:** The control garment.

The plainest piece in the Collection, and the quietest. A classic boxer short in a quiet weave, cut to sit below the waist and above the conversation. No branding at the hem. No visible argument. It is the garment the panel wears when the variable must be removed and the day must still be documented.

### The Backchannel

**Positioning:** A discreet boxer brief.

For the days that require a closer brief. A clean, close cut that stays where it is put and says nothing at the waistband. Designed for travel, long tables, and rooms where the outer layer is doing most of the work. The Institute regards discretion as a form of accuracy.

### The Reading Room

**Positioning:** A lounge pant for long-form sitting.

A soft, straight lounge pant for the reading-aloud championship and the quieter hours after it. Elastic waist, deep pockets, a leg that ends without ceremony. Worn with the Quorum or without company. The Institute has observed that most important sentences are read twice, and most trousers are not built for the second reading.

### The Protocol

**Positioning:** The evening short.

A boxer short for occasions that begin after the tie is removed. Slightly higher at the waist, quieter in the finish, with a stripe that is felt rather than seen. Appropriate for guest rooms, late trains, and the first honest hour of the day. The Protocol is not formalwear. It is what formalwear becomes when the door closes.

## 7. Voice Guidelines

- **Write like an institution, not a brand.** Use the vocabulary of research — index, panel, method, observation — to describe ordinary garments. Do: "The sample is small by design." Don't: "Our boxers are scientifically proven to be the comfiest."
- **Understate the joke.** The comedy comes from the straight face, not the punchline. Do: "Power is never fully dressed." Don't: "Underwear for world leaders — lol."
- **Prefer observation to adjectives.** A specific condition is more credible, and funnier, than a superlative. Do: "Observed seated for four hours; no adjustment required." Don't: "Unbelievably, ridiculously soft."
- **Keep the founder factual.** Use only the documented career. Do not invent awards, customers, partnerships, or political claims. Do: "His team held a May Day rally inside Minecraft." Don't: "Ilya advised every major world leader."
- **Never explain the satire.** If a line needs a wink, cut the wink. Do: "Findings are published, worn, and revised." Don't: "Get it? It's a think tank for boxers."
