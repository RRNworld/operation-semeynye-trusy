## 1. `/research` — Research

**H1:** Research

**Standfirst:** Field notes on fit, fabric, and the conditions in which the first layer is actually worn. The sample is small by design; the conclusions are provisional.

**Body:**

Research at the Institute begins with a person who has been sitting for four hours. Not a model, not a mirror, not a fitting room with the door closed. The garment is studied where it is asked to hold: under tailoring, on a night train, in a warm room, after the meeting has run late.

A finding is a sentence the evidence can carry. Where the evidence is thin, the sentence is shorter. Where the finding changes, the correction stays in the record beside the claim it replaced. This is slower than opinion and less satisfying than a slogan, which is why the Institute keeps doing it.

The programme is not market research. A preference may be recorded, but it is not promoted to evidence. The panel observes conditions, repeats them where it can, and states the limits before it states the conclusion. The Index is an annual edition with periodic revisions; the research record is continuous.

### Current studies

The current programme is deliberately small. Each study addresses one question and runs until the panel can describe the result without adjectives. Studies are listed with their status and their limits; a study without a stated limit is not ready to be listed.

**Study 01 — The Seated Hour.** A continuing observation of waistband behaviour across a working day. Assessed seated, standing, and in transition. The panel records adjustments, not impressions. Status: in progress; observations recorded through Q4 2026.

**Study 02 — Laundry and Legibility.** What survives the wash: seam geometry, hem behaviour, and the quiet arithmetic of shrinkage. Conducted with the Institute's standard wash protocol and recorded against the care reference published with each garment. Status: in progress; first review January 2027.

**Study 03 — The Hotel Drawer.** An occasional study of garments encountered away from home. The sample is opportunistic and the conclusions are correspondingly modest. Status: occasional; next review January 2027.

### Method

Each study names its question before it begins. Conditions are repeated, observations are logged, and the panel's median is preferred to its mean, because the mean rewards enthusiasm. A result is published with its method attached; a result without a method is an anecdote.

Sessions are conducted seated where possible. The panel is asked to describe the day, not the garment. Adjustments are counted without interpretation. Where a garment performs well, the panel is asked what else was true: the room, the chair, the hour, the outer layer. Comfort is a claim; composure is evidence.

The Institute does not confuse a laboratory with a life. A garment that works only in a fitting room has been flattered, not tested. The method is designed to make flattery difficult and to leave the finding open to revision.

### The standing panel

The panel is small by design. It is composed of people who spend their days in rooms not of their choosing: tailors, archivists, logistics coordinators, and others who work seated for long hours. Members serve for 12 months and are recorded in the panel register.

Assessors are issued garments without labels where the protocol allows. Most can identify the maker anyway. The Institute retains the blind protocol as a ritual rather than a control, and reports the result with the appropriate humility.

The panel is not a focus group. It does not deliberate toward a preference, and it does not produce consensus for publication. It produces observations, which the Institute edits into findings. The distinction is maintained in the archive.

### Archive

The archive keeps every dispatch, observation, and revision since the mandate changed. Records are dated and retained. A superseded finding remains legible beside the finding that replaced it; the Institute regards the pair as more honest than the correction alone.

The archive is open to readers. Access notes and retention terms are recorded in the Institute's archive register. Material that cannot be published is described in general terms rather than silently omitted.

### Corrections

Corrections are published, not absorbed. A correction states what was claimed, what is now known, and what changed. It is linked from the original page and from the correction record.

A finding is revised for evidence, arithmetic, or a change in elastic. It is not revised for taste. To submit a correction, write to boxers@rrn.world with the subject line "Correction" and the dispatch or study reference.

**Empty state (current studies):** No studies are currently accepting observation. The panel is between questions; the archive remains open.

**CTA:** Primary: Read the latest dispatch. Secondary: Review the archive.

## 2. `/osint` — Open Source

**H1:** Open Source

**Standfirst:** Open-source observation of the garment in public: labels, care instructions, retail listings, and the visible seam. The Institute reviews public material only and publishes what can be verified.

**Body:**

Open Source is the Institute's public-material desk. It reads what a garment says about itself in the open: the label, the care card, the retail listing, the seam visible at the hem. The desk does not need access to a factory to describe what a hem is doing.

The method is documentary. We collect public material, record where it was found, and separate what is stated from what is inferred. A claim without a source is not published. A source without a date is treated as incomplete.

The desk does not assess people and does not collect personal material. It studies garments, labels, retail copy, and the small inconsistencies between them. Open Source is a reading practice, not a surveillance practice.

Where a claim cannot be verified, the brief says so. The Institute prefers an incomplete record to a confident one, and it would rather publish a limit than a guess.

### Open-source briefs

Briefs are shorter than studies and are published when a public record is complete enough to describe. Each carries a source list and a statement of what could not be verified.

**Brief 001 — What the label declares.** A reading of fibre content, origin statements, and care symbols as public claims. Status: in preparation.

**Brief 002 — The retail listing and the garment.** Where listing language and the visible object diverge. Status: in preparation.

**Brief 003 — The visible seam.** Construction as it can be observed without disassembly. Status: in preparation.

Briefs are numbered in the order they are opened, not in order of importance.

### Verification

Every brief carries a source list. Sources are public pages, label photographs, care cards, and listings captured on the date recorded in each source list. The list records what was available at the time of reading.

Verification has two steps: confirm the material exists, then confirm the claim it supports. If only the first is possible, the brief states "material located, claim unverified." The phrase is not a failure; it is a finding about the record.

Where a retail listing changes after capture, the brief keeps the captured version and notes the change. The Institute does not update a source silently.

### Ethics and limits

The desk excludes personal accounts, private correspondence, and material that could identify a private individual. It does not contact individuals for comment. Public does not mean unrestricted.

The limits are stated in each brief: what was available, what was not, and what the desk declined to infer. A brief that cannot state its limits is not published.

Open Source does not use non-public material, does not commission access, and does not describe itself as an investigative service. It reads the record that is already there.

### Reading list

The reading list collects the public references used by the desk: labelling conventions, care terminology, retail copy, and the occasional manufacturer glossary. Entries are descriptive, not endorsements.

The current list is maintained on this page and updated as sources are added.

### Archive

Older briefs remain in the record with their original source lists. A brief is not deleted when it is superseded; it is marked as superseded and kept.

Archive access and retention terms are recorded in the Institute's archive register.

## 5. `/sources` — Citations

**H1:** Citations

**Standfirst:** The Institute cites what it observes. This page records the sources behind each Index edition, field note, and correction, including material tests, care data, and editorial correspondence.

**Body:**

The Institute cites what it observes. A citation records the material, the date it was consulted, and the claim it supports. It is not a bibliography of influence; it is the record of what was in front of the assessor when the finding was made.

The page is organized by publication rather than by author. Where a source cannot be published, the entry states the restriction and the reason in general terms. An absent citation is a finding, not a gap to be filled.

Citations are added as an edition is prepared and frozen at publication. Corrections to a citation are dated and kept with the original entry. The Institute does not revise a citation to make a finding look better.

A citation is published only when the reference is complete: author, edition, table, line, and the claim supported.

### Index 2026

The 2026 bibliography records the materials consulted for the annual edition. Entries are numbered in the order they are cited in the edition and frozen at publication.

**Empty state (bibliography not yet released):** No bibliography entries have been released for this edition. The bibliography is in preparation.

### Field notes

Field-note citations are lighter: a source list at the foot of the note, with the conditions of observation and any limitation. Sources are numbered in the order they appear, not in order of importance. The Institute does not rank its evidence.

**Entry template:** Field note reference — Source title. Date. Condition recorded: condition observed. Limitation: stated limitation.

Citations are repeated in full at first use in each publication. The Institute does not carry a reference across publications on trust.

### Corrections

A correction cites the original claim, the corrected claim, and the material that prompted the change. It is linked from the correction record and from the original page.

Superseded citations remain visible and marked. They are not deleted; a reader should be able to see what the Institute believed it knew.

To submit a citation correction: boxers@rrn.world with the subject "Citation correction."

### Citation style

The Institute uses a house style: author, edition, table, line, and the claim supported. Where the source is a garment, the label and care card are cited as objects.

`Author. Edition. Table. Line. Supports: section.`

Quotations retain original spelling and punctuation. Translations are marked as translations. Where no public source exists, the entry says so.

### Archive

Previous bibliographies remain available with their editions. Citations are not transferred between editions; a source reused in a later edition is cited again.

Archive access and retention terms are recorded in the Institute's archive register.

Support funds independent research and the maintenance of the Index. It does not purchase influence, ranking, or editorial consideration. The Institute is small and intends to remain so; support is used to keep the record, not to enlarge the language around it.

Independent research is cheap to describe and expensive to maintain. A study requires time, a panel, and the willingness to publish a result that is smaller than expected. Support also maintains the archive and the correction record, which are the parts of an institute that no one photographs.

**H1:** Fellows

**Standfirst:** The Institute appoints fellows for fixed terms to study specific questions in the first layer. Each profile records the remit, the method, and the disclosures.

**Body:**

Fellowship is an appointment, not a title of honour. A fellow is given a question, a term, and the obligation to publish the method as well as the finding.

The Institute appoints fellows for fixed terms. Appointments are recorded with a remit and a disclosure. There is no roster inherited from a previous mandate, and no profile is imported without consent.

A fellowship does not imply agreement with the Institute's conclusions, and it does not confer editorial authority over the Index. The fellow's work is published under the fellow's name and the Institute's method.

Where no appointments have been approved, the page says so. The Institute prefers an empty list to a borrowed one.

### Current fellows

Current fellows are listed with their remit and term. Profiles are published only after appointment, disclosure, and consent.

### Fellowship

A fellowship is a fixed-term appointment to a defined question in the first layer. The remit is written before the appointment and published with it. The fellow is expected to produce one field note and a closing report during the term.

Fellows work according to a fixed-term, part-time arrangement agreed at appointment. The Institute sets out the terms, the support, and the limits in the Fellowship Policy.

The Institute does not accept unsolicited applications outside published application windows. Application windows, where they exist, are published under Appointments.

Appointments are made by the Institute's appointments committee for a term of 12 months. The appointment records the remit, the method, and any conflict. Reappointment is at the Institute's discretion under the Fellowship Policy.

Each fellow discloses financial interests, institutional affiliations, and any relevant relationships before the term begins and updates the record when circumstances change. Disclosures are published with the profile.

The Institute publishes disclosures in the form approved by the fellow. It does not publish personal information beyond the approved biography and disclosure.

### 15.3 `/about/osint` — Open Source Ethics and Verification

**H1:** Open Source ethics and verification

**Standfirst:** Open Source reads the garment in public: labels, care data, retail listings, and the visible seam. It publishes only what the public record can support.

**Body:**

Open Source is a documentary practice. The desk collects material that anyone can inspect: a care label, a fibre declaration, a retail page, a seam photographed at the hem. It records where the material was found and what claim it can support. It does not infer motive, character, or private circumstance.

Verification has two steps. First, the material must exist in the public record. Second, the claim must follow from it. Where only the first step is possible, the brief is published as material located, claim unverified. The Institute prefers an incomplete record to a confident one.

Ethics are procedural. The desk excludes personal accounts, private correspondence, and material that could identify a private individual. It does not contact individuals for comment or republish removed material. Public does not mean unrestricted.

The limits are stated in every brief: what was available, what was not, and what the desk declined to infer. Open Source is a reading practice, not a surveillance practice. It studies garments, not people.

**What the desk reads**

Public labels, care data, retail listings, and visible seams.

**What it does not do**

No person-focused material, no private correspondence, no non-public access.

**How claims are marked**

Verified, material located, or unverified. The phrase is part of the finding.

**H1:** The Method

**Standfirst:** Fit, fabric, sizing, and care. The Institute's guidance for wearing and maintaining the first layer.

Fit begins at the waist and ends at the leg opening. A waistband should hold without governing; a leg should clear the thigh without billowing.
