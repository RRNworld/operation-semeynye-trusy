# Portugal — Boxer Sovereignty Profile 2026

Portugal places first of sixty, and the panel's most complete profile is a garment shaped by Atlantic humidity, domestic drying, and a repair culture that treats replacement as a last resort.

**Composite:** 93.8 · **Rank:** 1 of 60 · **Tier:** Sovereign · **Region:** Europe

*Scope: This profile assesses garment conditions only. It does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty.*

## Profile at a glance

| Dimension | Score | Observation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Waistband Sovereignty | 96 | A narrow elastic, stitched flat; no roll recorded across four seated hours. |
| Thermal Independence | 92 | Stable in unheated stone rooms; recovers quickly after a balcony crossing. |
| Structural Integrity | 95 | Seams and gusset unchanged after repeated line drying; the hem kept its line. |
| Diplomatic Comfort | 94 | Disappeared under a jacket and a belted coat without requesting attention. |
| Nocturnal Security | 93 | Minimal displacement overnight; the waistband remembered its position. |
| Pattern Jurisdiction | 90 | Plain weave preferred; the panel found no claim the cloth could not support. |
| Institutional Memory | 94 | Color held after frequent washing; softness increased without loss of shape. |

## National observation

Portugal enters the 2026 Edition first, and the panel has described why without overstating it. The assessment begins with humidity. Along the Atlantic coast, air that is rarely dry or cold imposes a discipline on cotton: a garment is expected to dry without stiffening and to be worn again before the wardrobe has recovered. The panel recorded a preference for plain weaves, dense enough to hold a seam but light enough to dry indoors.

Domestic architecture supports the habit. Rooms in older stock tend to be cool in summer and difficult to heat evenly in winter, so clothing is added and removed in increments rather than in a single decision. The panel observed that this favors a waistband of modest tension and a leg cut that does not require rearrangement when a second layer arrives.

Laundry is frequent and largely domestic. Machines are small, drying is done on a line or a rack, and garments return to service quickly, which places the burden on seams and elastic rather than on finish. Repair remains ordinary: hems are taken up, waistbands are replaced, and a garment with a history is not automatically retired.

Retail contributes a conservative instinct. The panel noted quiet patterns and a suspicion of decoration that cannot survive a wash. Textile heritage was visible less in ornament than in expectations of hand and drape. The result is a profile without a weak dimension: 93.8, first place by a margin the panel declines to call comfortable.

## Field note

Field note — Lisbon, March. Assessment conducted in a third-floor apartment with painted stone walls, no central heating, and relative humidity of 78 per cent at 08:00. Two assessors wore the sample through a seated morning of paperwork, a walk to a nearby market, and a second seated period after lunch. The garment was washed by hand in the evening and dried on a rack beside an open window. By morning the waistband had recovered without assistance and the hem had kept its line. One adjustment was recorded during the walk and attributed to the coat. No further comment was entered.

## Scope and limitations

This profile assesses garment conditions only. It does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty, and no finding here should be read as a comment on any of them. Observations come from a small standing panel wearing ordinary retail garments under the conditions recorded above. Scores are internal IBI medians, expressed to one decimal place, and are not measurements of the country, its people, or its weather. The sample is small by design; conclusions are provisional and may be revised after further wears, a change of elastic, or a correction demanded by arithmetic.

# Russia — Boxer Sovereignty Profile 2026

Russia places third of sixty, with the field's strongest institutional memory and a profile organized around the heating season rather than the street.

**Composite:** 92.1 · **Rank:** 3 of 60 · **Tier:** Sovereign · **Region:** Europe

*Scope: This profile assesses garment conditions only. It does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty.*

## Profile at a glance

| Dimension | Score | Observation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Waistband Sovereignty | 94 | Held under winter layers; returned to position after four transitions at the door. |
| Thermal Independence | 91 | Performed in heated rooms; slower to settle in the unheated stairwell. |
| Structural Integrity | 93 | Seams survived indoor drying near a radiator without pucker. |
| Diplomatic Comfort | 90 | Acceptable under a suit; slight awareness at the waist after lunch. |
| Nocturnal Security | 92 | No displacement recorded across two nights in a shared room. |
| Pattern Jurisdiction | 88 | Prints chosen for the interior; the repeat survived the hem without argument. |
| Institutional Memory | 95 | Highest retention in the field; still legible after a long rotation. |

## National observation

Russia's profile is shaped by an indoor climate that is warm for much of the year and a doorway that is not. The panel observed that assessment conditions divide sharply between heated rooms and the short, cold passages between them, and that the garment is judged less by how it performs in either condition than by how quickly it recovers when the coat comes off. A waistband compressed under winter layers must return without a meeting.

The heating season sets a domestic rhythm. Radiators run high, windows are opened for air, and laundry dries indoors for months at a time. The panel recorded that garments are washed at moderate temperatures, dried near heat, and returned to rotation quickly. This favors cotton finished without stiffness and seams that do not pucker after repeated cycles.

Domestic architecture contributes. Apartments are compact, storage is vertical, and clothing is kept in rotation rather than in reserve. The panel noted a strong institutional memory: garments remain in use for years, waistbands are replaced rather than discarded, and the record of wear is visible without being treated as failure. Retention scored 95, the highest in the field; pattern scored slightly lower, with prints chosen for the rooms in which they are worn rather than for an audience.

Retail and repair remain closely linked. Small ateliers and market stalls are part of the ordinary route, and alteration is treated as maintenance rather than correction. Composite: 92.1, third of sixty.

## Field note

Field note — Moscow, January. Assessment conducted across two days in a heated apartment at 23–24°C, with a single unheated stairwell and an outdoor temperature of −14°C at 09:00. Two assessors dressed and undressed four times in succession, recording the waistband's behavior after each transition. The sample was laundered at 30°C, dried on a rack above a radiator, and worn again after eleven hours. No displacement was recorded overnight; one adjustment followed the second outdoor transition; the hem showed no visible change. The assessor noted that the garment was warmer than expected in the room and cooler than expected at the door, and entered this as a compliment.

## Scope and limitations

This profile assesses garment conditions only; it does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty. The assessment was conducted during the heating season in a single city, and the panel makes no claim that it represents the country as a whole. Field conditions are recorded so that readers may judge how far the findings travel. Scores are internal IBI medians and are not measurements of the state or its institutions. Conclusions are provisional and may be revised after further wears, a change of elastic, or arithmetic.

# Germany — Boxer Sovereignty Profile 2026

Germany places fifth of sixty, where shared laundry rooms, a literal reading of care labels, and replacement before failure produce the Edition's most disciplined profile.

**Composite:** 91.1 · **Rank:** 5 of 60 · **Tier:** Sovereign · **Region:** Europe

*Scope: This profile assesses garment conditions only. It does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty.*

## Profile at a glance

| Dimension | Score | Observation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Waistband Sovereignty | 93 | Recovered fully after a 40°C cycle; no adjustment during a working day. |
| Thermal Independence | 89 | Even performance in controlled rooms; less convincing in the drying room. |
| Structural Integrity | 94 | Elastic replaced before failure; seams unchanged after fourteen wears. |
| Diplomatic Comfort | 91 | Sat quietly under wool trousers through two long sittings. |
| Nocturnal Security | 90 | Undisturbed overnight; the panel recorded nothing and meant it. |
| Pattern Jurisdiction | 85 | Plain weave and small repeat; the claim was modest and supported. |
| Institutional Memory | 92 | Care label consulted; the garment retained shape across the week. |

## National observation

Germany's assessment is dominated by infrastructure rather than climate. The panel observed that the garment spends most of its life in rooms whose temperature is controlled, and that the interesting variation occurs in the shared laundry room. Buildings of a certain age provide a common washing space, a drying room, and a schedule; garments are laundered on an appointed day and returned in the interval the schedule allows.

This produces a profile in which structural integrity (94) leads the technical scores. Garments are washed less frequently than in warmer markets but more consistently, at temperatures chosen from the care label rather than habit. The panel noted that labels are read, that elastic is replaced before it fails, and that a loose seam is treated as a maintenance item rather than a reason to retire it.

Domestic architecture reinforces the pattern. Deep window reveals, tilt-and-turn ventilation, and a clear separation between wet and dry zones mean that drying is planned rather than improvised. The panel observed that a garment which dries evenly indoors keeps its geometry, and this is valued.

Pattern Jurisdiction is the lowest score (85), not because the market lacks design but because the panel recorded a preference for plain weaves and small repeats that do not require explanation. Retail supports this: the assessment route ran through department stores and specialist shops where fit is discussed in measurements rather than mood. Composite: 91.1, fifth of sixty. The panel described the result as "correct," which it intends as praise.

## Field note

Field note — Leipzig, February. Assessment conducted in an apartment block of the early twentieth century, with a shared laundry room in the basement, a drying room, and a posted schedule. Ambient conditions: 19°C and 52 per cent relative humidity in the laundry, 22°C in the apartment. The sample was washed at 40°C on the appointed day, dried on a rack for fourteen hours, and worn for a working day that included two long sittings and a tram journey. No adjustment was recorded. The waistband had recovered its circumference by morning. The assessor noted that the care label was consulted before both washes, and that this was the most interesting part of the day.

## Scope and limitations

This profile assesses garment conditions only. It does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty, and the panel asks that the distinction be kept in view. The assessment took place in one building type, in one season, with laundry conditions that are not universal. Scores are internal IBI medians, not national indicators, and the sample is small by design. Findings may be revised after further wears, a change of elastic, or a correction demanded by arithmetic. The Institute publishes corrections rather than absorbing them.

# Italy — Boxer Sovereignty Profile 2026

Italy places fourth of sixty, and offers the Edition's clearest case for tailoring: a garment judged by how it disappears under the jacket above it.

**Composite:** 91.6 · **Rank:** 4 of 60 · **Tier:** Sovereign · **Region:** Europe

*Scope: This profile assesses garment conditions only. It does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty.*

## Profile at a glance

| Dimension | Score | Observation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Waistband Sovereignty | 91 | Fitted rather than elastic-led; one adjustment after the first sitting. |
| Thermal Independence | 90 | Stable under a jacket in a cool room; less so in unheated stone. |
| Structural Integrity | 92 | Alteration left no visible trace; seams kept their line after washing. |
| Diplomatic Comfort | 94 | The profile's strongest score; the garment disappeared into the trouser line. |
| Nocturnal Security | 89 | Slight torsion recorded and attributed to the sheet, not the garment. |
| Pattern Jurisdiction | 95 | Stripes aligned at the seam; the repeat governed its own territory. |
| Institutional Memory | 91 | Softened without losing structure; color remained even at the hem. |

## National observation

Italy's profile is best understood through the alteration shop. The panel observed an unusually dense infrastructure for adjusting clothes, and noted that this shapes what is expected of a garment before it is ever worn. A waistband is not accepted as finished because it was sewn; it is accepted because it has been fitted. The score for diplomatic comfort (94) reflects this: the garment was assessed under jackets and at long tables, and was expected to disappear into the line of the trouser without being asked.

Textile heritage is regional and specific. The panel recorded tightly woven cottons, small geometric repeats, and a willingness to use pattern as structure rather than decoration. Pattern Jurisdiction (95) is the highest score in the profile, and the only dimension in which the panel described the garment as making a claim and then supporting it. Stripes aligned at the seam; repeats survived the hem.

Laundry is domestic and frequent, with drying on balconies and racks according to season. The panel noted that ironing is treated as part of care rather than as correction, and that a garment which requires ironing to look finished is not penalized if it repays the effort.

The profile is consistent across all seven dimensions, with a characteristic emphasis: comfort under tailoring, integrity after alteration, and pattern that knows its own edges. Composite: 91.6, fourth of sixty.

## Field note

Field note — Bologna, October. Assessment conducted in a fitting room behind a small alteration workshop, at 21°C and 58 per cent relative humidity, with two hours of seated measurement and an afternoon under a wool jacket. The sample was worn through a lunch that ran long and a walk through the arcades in light rain. No adjustment was recorded at the waist; one was recorded at the hem of the outer trouser and was not attributed to the garment. The waistband was inspected after the jacket came off and had kept its line. The tailor present declined to comment, then commented favorably on the seam allowance.

## Scope and limitations

This profile assesses garment conditions only; it does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty. Observations were made in a single alteration workshop and its immediate surroundings, and the panel does not present them as representative of the country. The garment was assessed after fitting, a condition the method records rather than removes. Scores are internal IBI medians, not measurements of the state or its people. Conclusions are provisional and may be revised after further wears, a change of elastic, or arithmetic.

# France — Boxer Sovereignty Profile 2026

France places sixth of sixty, where the household linen cupboard sets the standard and pattern is expected to behave like a private register.

**Composite:** 90.4 · **Rank:** 6 of 60 · **Tier:** Sovereign · **Region:** Europe

*Scope: This profile assesses garment conditions only. It does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty.*

## Profile at a glance

| Dimension | Score | Observation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Waistband Sovereignty | 90 | Held through a long lunch; one adjustment recorded at the table. |
| Thermal Independence | 89 | Comfortable in a cool apartment; slower to recover after air drying. |
| Structural Integrity | 91 | Seams and hem survived gentle cycles; the waistband retained its line. |
| Diplomatic Comfort | 92 | Behaved well under tailoring and at a table that ran late. |
| Nocturnal Security | 88 | Minor displacement overnight; no comment entered. |
| Pattern Jurisdiction | 93 | Small checks aligned; the pattern made no public statement. |
| Institutional Memory | 90 | Kept its color and hand; the record of wear was visible but orderly. |

## National observation

France's profile begins with the household linen cupboard, an object the panel was not expecting to matter. Assessment repeatedly returned to the idea that a garment is kept in relation to other textiles: sheets, towels, napkins, the inherited stack that defines what is considered decent in a domestic interior. The panel observed that this raises expectations of hand and finish without raising expectations of display. A boxer short is expected to be quiet, to wash well, and to belong to a set of things that are kept.

Laundry practice is gentle and domestic. Machines are small, cycles are long, and drying is frequently done in air rather than in heat. The panel recorded that garments are washed at low temperatures, smoothed by hand, and hung with attention to the line. This favors cotton with some body and a waistband that does not rely on aggressive elastic for authority.

Pattern Jurisdiction (93) is the profile's strongest dimension. The panel noted stripes, small checks, and repeats that behave like a private register rather than a public statement. The pattern is expected to survive hemming and to align at the seam, and it usually does.

Retail is specialized rather than expansive. The assessment route included haberdashery counters and small shops where fit is discussed quietly and alteration is offered as a matter of course. The result is a profile that leads no dimension decisively: 90.4, sixth of sixty. The panel described it as "well kept," and meant the garment and the habit.

## Field note

Field note — Lyon, April. Assessment conducted in a fourth-floor apartment during steady rain, with the heating on low and a relative humidity of 66 per cent at 09:00. The sample was worn through a seated morning, a short errand under an umbrella, and an evening at a table that ran late. It was washed at 30°C, smoothed by hand, and hung on a rack in the spare room overnight. The waistband was still faintly damp at 07:00 but had kept its shape; by noon it was dry and unchanged. One adjustment was recorded at dinner and attributed to the chair.

## Scope and limitations

This profile assesses garment conditions only. It does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty, and no score should be read as a verdict on either. The assessment drew on domestic laundry conditions in one city during a wet month; other households would produce other observations. Scores are internal IBI medians, expressed to one decimal place, and the sample is small by design. Findings may be revised after further wears, a change of elastic, or a correction demanded by arithmetic. Corrections are published and kept.

# United Kingdom — Boxer Sovereignty Profile 2026

The United Kingdom places seventh of sixty, in a profile shaped by draughts, slow drying, and a repair habit that keeps garments in the drawer long after their best appearance.

**Composite:** 90.4 · **Rank:** 7 of 60 · **Tier:** Sovereign · **Region:** Europe

*Scope: This profile assesses garment conditions only. It does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty.*

## Profile at a glance

| Dimension | Score | Observation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Waistband Sovereignty | 91 | Recovered after a damp night; one adjustment the following morning. |
| Thermal Independence | 88 | The lowest technical score; the building, not the garment, set the terms. |
| Structural Integrity | 90 | Hems held; the waistband showed the memory of a radiator. |
| Diplomatic Comfort | 91 | Sat without complaint under a suit in an unheated room. |
| Nocturnal Security | 89 | One displacement recorded; attributed to the cold room. |
| Pattern Jurisdiction | 92 | Stripes and small checks chosen for the wearer rather than the room. |
| Institutional Memory | 93 | Kept long after best appearance; the panel did not treat this as failure. |

## National observation

The United Kingdom's assessment is a study in draughts. The panel observed that the housing stock imposes conditions no garment can control: sash windows that do not close flush, suspended floors, radiators beneath windows, and a bathroom that is the coldest room. Thermal Independence (88) is therefore the lowest technical score in the profile, not because the garment fails but because the building asks more of it than the climate would.

Laundry is domestic and improvised. Machines are commonly in the kitchen, drying happens on a rack or over a door, and the airing cupboard retains a ceremonial role. The panel recorded that garments are washed frequently, dried slowly, and returned with a faint awareness of damp. Cotton that dries without stiffness is valued; so is a waistband that does not hold the memory of a radiator.

Repair is ordinary. Hems are stitched, buttons are replaced, and a garment is kept for gardening or decorating before being discarded. Institutional Memory (93) reflects this: the panel noted that garments remain in the drawer long after their best appearance, and that this is not regarded as failure.

Pattern Jurisdiction (92) is the quiet surprise. The panel recorded stripes, small checks, and woven patterns chosen for the wearer rather than the room. Retail remains local: high streets, department stores, and specialist shops where fit is discussed without ceremony. Composite: 90.4, seventh of sixty. The profile reads as weatherproof in the ways that matter, and honest about the ways it is not.

## Field note

Field note — Sheffield, November. Assessment conducted in a Victorian terraced house with sash windows, a gas fire in the front room, and no heating in the back bedroom. Ambient conditions: 17°C and 64 per cent relative humidity at 07:00, rising to 20°C by evening. The sample was worn through a seated morning, a walk in steady rain, and an evening on a drying rack. The waistband was recorded as damp at 22:00 after hanging near a window; by morning it was dry and had recovered its shape. One adjustment was noted at the waist after the walk. The assessor attributed it to the weather and declined to elaborate.

## Scope and limitations

This profile assesses garment conditions only; it does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty. The panel assessed the garment in one housing type, in one season, and makes no claim that the conditions are universal. Damp and draught are recorded as assessment conditions, not as national characteristics. Scores are internal IBI medians, not measurements of the state or its people. Conclusions are provisional and may be revised after further wears, a change of elastic, or arithmetic. The Institute publishes corrections rather than absorbing them.

# United States — Boxer Sovereignty Profile 2026

The United States places tenth of sixty in the widest sample of the Edition, assessed across heated, cooled, and commercially laundered rooms.

**Composite:** 88.5 · **Rank:** 10 of 60 · **Tier:** Autonomous · **Region:** Americas

*Scope: This profile assesses garment conditions only. It does not assess governance, state capacity, or national sovereignty.*

## Profile at a glance

| Dimension | Score | Observation |

| Waistband Sovereignty | 90 | Generous elastic; no adjustment during a four-hour sitting. |
| Thermal Independence | 87 | Performed differently across heated and cooled rooms; averaged honestly. |
| Structural Integrity | 89 | Survived a hot dryer; the hem softened but did not fail. |
| Diplomatic Comfort | 88 | Acceptable under trousers; the waistband was noticeable after lunch. |

| Pattern Jurisdiction | 86 | Visible marks at the waistband treated as a claim, not fully supported. |

## National observation

The United States is the widest sample in the Edition and the least uniform. The panel assessed the garment in conditions that ranged from a heated apartment in a cold city to a cooled room in a humid one, and recorded that the same garment behaves differently across those rooms. Nocturnal Security (90) is strong. The panel recorded little displacement and attributed it to a generous cut. Composite: 88.5, tenth of sixty. The profile is not sovereign, but it is self-possessed, which the Index defines as a different and slightly more honest condition.

Field note — Chicago, April. Assessment conducted over two days: a heated apartment at 22°C and 38 per cent relative humidity, a commercial laundromat at 24°C, and a long seated train journey. The assessor noted that the laundromat was the warmest room of the day and that the garment behaved best there, a finding entered without interpretation.

Domestic heating is dry and consistent. The panel noted forced-air systems, sealed windows, and a house that is often warmer than its occupants require. This favors cotton with a stable hand and a waistband that does not depend on moisture to recover.

The panel recorded a preference for plain weaves and small functional patterns, and treated the absence of decoration as a decision rather than a default. ## National observation

Brazil's profile is a study of frequency. The panel recorded the highest washing frequency in the Edition, not because the garment is treated harshly but because the climate makes a second wear difficult to justify. Heat and humidity are not abstractions here; they enter the wardrobe. Thermal Independence (94) is the highest score in the profile, and the only dimension in which the panel recorded the garment improving as the day grew warmer.

Laundry is domestic and improvisational. The panel noted small machines, hand washing in utility sinks, and drying on lines, racks, and balcony rails. Sun is abundant and is treated as part of the method. The result is a garment that fades evenly and dries quickly, but also one whose seams are asked to survive a rhythm of washing considered excessive elsewhere. Structural Integrity (87) reflects that rhythm honestly.

Domestic architecture supports airflow. Rooms are opened rather than sealed, and the panel observed that a garment which dries on the body is not considered a failure of planning. Retail is varied: markets, small shops, and larger stores, with a strong preference for pattern that can be read at a distance. Pattern Jurisdiction (92) is the profile's second-highest score.

Diplomatic Comfort (85) is the weakest dimension, largely because cooled rooms impose a different set of expectations from the street. Composite: 88.0, eleventh of sixty. The finding describes a garment that understands the afternoon better than the meeting; the panel recorded this was not entirely a criticism.

It was washed by hand after the walk and dried on a line before sunset. The waistband required two adjustments during the afternoon and none after drying. The assessor recorded that the garment was more comfortable when the fan was on, and that this was not a finding about the fan.

The assessment took place in one coastal city during a hot month, with hand washing and line drying; other households would produce other observations. Scores are internal IBI medians, not measurements of the country or its people. The sample is small by design. Findings may be revised after further wears, a change of elastic, or arithmetic.

## National observation
